# How the pieces move

Every piece starts as a cob. Some become roks. The difference between them is
the difference between a piece that can only go forward and one that can go
anywhere, and almost every plan in Tarati turns on it.

## Cobs go forward

A cob moves one step to an adjacent point, and only toward the enemy. It can
never step sideways along a rank, and it can never retreat. Whatever a cob has
walked past is behind it for good.

This gives a cob a strange, committing quality. A cob is a promise you cannot
take back. Push it up the board and it gains ground and loses options at the
same rate, until it either promotes on the far home base or runs out of squares.

There is one exception, and it is deliberate. A cob standing on one of its own
home points may move in any direction, forward or back or across. Home is the
one place a cob is not yet committed. In practice this means your back rank can
answer a threat that a cob out in the field never could, so there is a real cost
to emptying your own base too early.

## Roks go anywhere

A cob that reaches the opponent's home base is promoted to a rok, and a rok
moves one step in any direction. Forward, backward, sideways, all the same.

That freedom is worth more than the extra material. A rok can retreat to defend
a point under threat, cross the board to attack from a side the enemy has left
open, and, above all, leave a square and come back to it. The striking chapter
showed why coming back matters: you can only capture an enemy by arriving from a
line you did not already command, and a cob, which never turns around, can
almost never arrange that against a piece it is facing. A rok can. This is why
attacks in the corpus are so often led by a promoted piece.

## Dead cobs

Because a cob can only go forward, a cob with no forward left is stuck forever.

![The black cob on D2 is dead. It sits at the far edge with no square ahead of it, and it will never move again.](fig:7872786,6299904,532480,0|mark=D2)

That black cob is on D2, deep inside white's home, at the very end of the board.
A black cob advances toward this edge, so from here there is no "forward" at
all. Both its neighbours are occupied as well, but even on an open board it
could not move. It is what the rules call dead.

Notice how it got there, because it is a rule worth knowing. A cob that reaches
the opponent's home by advancing would promote. This one did not promote, so it
did not arrive by advancing. It was a white cob, sitting on its own home point,
struck and flipped to black where it stood. A piece captured on its own base
keeps its rank instead of promoting, which is usually a mercy for the defender.
Here it is the opposite: the flip stranded a cob at the one edge of the board
where a cob is worthless.

A dead piece is not quite nothing. If a player has no legal move anywhere but
holds a dead cob, they are allowed to promote it on the spot so that it can move
after all, which is the only time you will see the `D2-D2` move. But a dead cob
you are not forced to promote is dead weight, and the endgame chapter shows how
often a game is decided by which side is carrying more of it.
